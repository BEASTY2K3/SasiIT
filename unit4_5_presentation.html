Performance Metrics • Topic 59

Internet Speed Metrics: Mbps vs. MB/s & Latency

Demystifying ISP marketing: the 8-bit math difference between Megabits and Megabytes, ping latency, and jitter during video calls.

Everyday Analogy Pipe Diameter vs. Water Volume vs. Travel Delay

Bandwidth (Mbps) is the width of a water pipe (how much water can flow through at once). Download Speed (MB/s) is the bucket of water filled per second. Latency / Ping is the time delay between turning the faucet knob and seeing the first drop emerge.

How It Works in 3 Simple Steps
1
The Divide-by-8 Rule: ISPs advertise speed in MegaBITS (small 'b', Mbps). File size is measured in MegaBYTES (capital 'B', MB). 8 bits = 1 Byte.
2
Real Speed Calculation: A 100 Mbps broadband plan downloads files at a maximum theoretical rate of `100 / 8 = 12.5 MB/s`.
3
Latency (Ping) & Jitter: Ping measures round-trip time in milliseconds (ms). Under 20 ms = ideal; over 150 ms = choppy audio and lag.
⚠
Troubleshooting Routine: When Zoom or Meet lags, don't just check speed; check Ping! High latency (ping > 100ms) causes awkward speech overlaps even on a 300 Mbps connection.
Figure 10 • Telecommunications Math & Latency Visualizer Vector SVG
Key Takeaway: 1 Byte = 8 bits. 100 Mbps Plan = 12.5 MB/s File Download. Ping = Real-time responsiveness.
